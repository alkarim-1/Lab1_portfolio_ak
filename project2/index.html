Project 2

Persuasive or Deceptive Visualization?

Ali Karim — alkarim@ucsd.edu

Proposition

Countries have made substantial progress in reducing greenhouse gas emissions since 1990.

Data Source: International Monetary Fund (IMF), greenhouse gas emissions excluding land-use change and forestry, 1990–2023.

FOR the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale

  • I selected the ten countries and territories with the largest percentage reductions to emphasize examples of progress. By focusing on the greatest reductions, the visualization supports the argument that substantial progress has been made without showing countries where emissions increased.
  • I used a horizontal bar chart sorted from largest to smallest reduction. This makes the differences easy to compare, while placing the strongest reductions at the top immediately draws the reader's attention.
  • I used a green gradient to reinforce the idea of environmental progress, with darker shades emphasizing larger reductions. I also included percentage labels at the end of each bar so readers can quickly identify the improvements without estimating values from the axis.
  • I calculated percentage changes between 1990 and 2023 rather than comparing total emissions. This allows countries with different starting emissions levels to be compared, although it does not show their overall contribution to global emissions.

AGAINST the Proposition

Design Decisions and Rationale

  • I used a time-series line chart to show how global greenhouse gas emissions have changed since 1990. Unlike the first visualization, this chart focuses on worldwide emissions rather than individual countries, providing a broader perspective.
  • I used a dark red line and light red shading beneath it to emphasize the upward trend. The red color creates a sense of concern and contrasts with the green tones used in the supporting visualization.
  • I added a prominent annotation showing the 58.6% increase since 1990. Positioning it near the most recent data point draws attention to the overall growth and reinforces the opposing argument.
  • I aggregated emissions across countries to show the global total, using million metric tons of CO2 equivalent. This emphasizes the worldwide trend rather than highlighting individual countries that achieved reductions.

Final Reflection

Both visualizations use greenhouse gas emissions data from 1990 to 2023, but they tell two different stories. The first visualization focuses on the ten countries and territories with the largest percentage reductions, showing that substantial progress has been made in certain places. The second visualization looks at global emissions over the same period and shows that emissions have continued to increase. Although both charts use the same underlying dataset, the way the data is selected and presented creates two opposing arguments.

I found the first visualization more persuasive because the horizontal bars, green color gradient, and percentage labels make the reductions easy to understand. However, it is also potentially misleading because it only highlights countries with the largest reductions while excluding those where emissions increased. The second visualization provides a broader perspective, but the red color and prominent percentage annotation make the increase appear particularly concerning. It also does not show which countries contributed most to the increase or which countries have made progress.

The biggest takeaway from this project is how much influence design decisions can have on the interpretation of data. Neither visualization needs to use false information to persuade the reader. Simply choosing which data to emphasize, how to display it, and what context to leave out can change the message significantly. This raises ethical concerns because viewers may accept a visualization's conclusion without questioning what information was excluded. It reinforced the importance of presenting data responsibly and providing enough context for readers to understand the full picture.
